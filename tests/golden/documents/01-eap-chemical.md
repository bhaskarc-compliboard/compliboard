## DOCUMENT TEXT

CASCADE SPECIALTY CHEMICALS, LLC
EMERGENCY ACTION PLAN
Portland Facility, 4410 NW Front Avenue, Portland, Oregon 97210
Revised February 2021

1. Purpose and scope

This Emergency Action Plan describes what employees of Cascade Specialty Chemicals do when there is a fire, chemical release, medical emergency, earthquake or other emergency at the Portland facility. It applies to all 42 employees at the Portland facility, including warehouse, drivers, laboratory and office staff, and to contractors and visitors while on site. The plan is kept in the front office and in the warehouse break room, and a copy is available to any employee on request.

2. Alarm system

The building has a monitored fire alarm system with audible horns and strobe lights in the warehouse, laboratory, loading dock and offices. The alarm is monitored 24 hours a day by Cascade Fire & Safety under a service contract. Manual pull stations are located at each exit door. The alarm is tested on the first Monday of each month by the facilities lead. A continuous horn means evacuate. Three short horns repeated means shelter in place because of an outdoor hazard.

3. Evacuation procedures

When the continuous alarm sounds, all employees stop work, secure nothing that requires more than a few seconds, and leave by the nearest marked exit. Exit routes are posted on the evacuation maps at each exit door and in Appendix A. Warehouse staff leave by the east dock doors or the north pedestrian door. Laboratory staff leave by the laboratory's own exterior door on the south wall. Office staff leave by the main entrance or the west stairwell. Do not use the freight lift. Do not return for belongings.

The assembly point is the far north end of the visitor parking lot, beside the flagpole, at least 150 feet from the building. Employees assigned as floor wardens (one per area, listed on the warden roster posted beside each evacuation map) sweep their area, close doors behind them, and report to the assembly point.

For a chemical release outdoors or a hazard that makes leaving the building unsafe, the shelter-in-place signal sounds. Employees move to the interior conference room or the laboratory corridor, close doors, and the facilities lead shuts down the building ventilation from the mechanical room panel.

4. Accounting for employees after evacuation

At the assembly point each floor warden takes a headcount of the people from their area against the daily sign-in sheet and the visitor log, which the receptionist brings out. Any person unaccounted for is reported at once to the incident coordinator at the assembly point, who passes the information to the fire department on arrival. No one re-enters the building until the fire department or the incident coordinator gives the all-clear. Evacuation drills are held twice a year, in spring and autumn.

5. Rescue and medical duties

Employees do not attempt rescue inside the building. First aid is given at the assembly point by the trained first-aid responders (two per shift, named on the warden roster), who bring the first-aid kit and the AED from the front office. An employee exposed to a chemical is taken to the emergency shower and eyewash at the loading dock if it can be reached safely, or to the assembly point, and the SDS for the product is brought to the responders. Emergency medical services are called for any injury beyond minor first aid.

6. Training and review

Every new employee is walked through this plan, the exit routes for their work area and the assembly point on their first day. Floor wardens and first-aid responders are trained annually. The plan is reviewed with all employees whenever it is changed and whenever an employee's responsibilities under it change. The facilities lead reviews the plan every January and updates the evacuation maps whenever the building layout changes.

Appendix A: Evacuation maps (warehouse, laboratory, offices)
Appendix B: Floor warden and first-aid responder roster (posted beside each evacuation map)
