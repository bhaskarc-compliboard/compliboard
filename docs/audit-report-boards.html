The audit report, drawn on the owner's own run

Draft 2, 4 November 2026 — the auditor's lines whole on the report; the checklists tab as white cards with a run table. Every line is from the Clackamas Blending & Supply run of 3 November — the owner's ten-line auditor checklist, the audit's own findings, reworded only where the new vocabulary applies. Four boards: the report as a full-width page; a document's reading as the one drawer over it; the Auditor checklists tab; and the Documents list with the new notes.

The five words a line can carry, as the customer sees them: On file · On file, gaps found · Out of date · Nothing on file · Not a document question. Suggestions carry a sixth label, Usually held — not seen, folded and never counted. Green boxes are decisions; amber boxes are the owner's calls.

1 · The report page — the Clackamas run, two rows open1280 × 1960

Full width, no drawer. The title is the auditor's own title for the checklist, and each row carries the auditor's line whole, word for word — once a person has this page they never need the auditor's file open beside it. The ten-cell strip is the picture: one cell per line in the auditor's order, coloured by its word, and the counts under it. Then one row per line — pill · line · requirement · the document it rests on — in Section A, B, C order, never regrouped. Two rows are drawn open: B2, the forklift roster, where today's "on file" hid two overdue operators and the fifth word now says so; and C1, a nothing-on-file line, which has no document and so no "line we read". An open row has three labelled parts — What we found · The line we read · What to do — and a fourth fold, Also noted, holding the caveats, the "proposed, not confirmed" asides and the dates that are not findings. No document handle (D6, D12) anywhere: the document's own title, as a link to its reading.

Decided 4 November (55–58). The report is a page, the reading is a drawer over it, the file is a button in the drawer. One row per checklist line in the auditor's order and numbering (50). The binder is built from a run, here and nowhere else; it numbers documents in order of first citation, one tab per document however many rows cite it.
Your reading — the five words. The pill says On file, gaps found in amber for A3 and B2. Alternatives were Gaps found (shorter, but does not say the document exists) and On file, incomplete (sounds like a judgement on the document). The suggestions sit in one folded grey line at the end, Usually held — not seen, outside the counts and the strip.

A rule for every list and report in the app (decision 59): status words and action links sit in fixed columns, left-aligned, so they line up down the page whatever sits beside them.
