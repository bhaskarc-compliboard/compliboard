## 1. In one paragraph

The Calendar is rebuilt from main on the branch `calendar-v2`, in the HR Workspace's frame, as the regulatory expert down the hall: a person asks "when is the Tier II report due?" and gets the answer with the document it came from. Every date the product finds, however it finds it, lands in one table, `calendar_events`, through one shared function, `proposeDate()`, and shows on the calendar at once, confirmed or not. A "Dates to confirm" tab shortens the work of saying yes. A person can send one date, or the whole calendar, to Google or Outlook. On the first of each month, one email says what is due. Fifteen steps; the quality tests are held for the finish line like every other section's.

## 2.1 What the Calendar is (the owner's vision, 15 October 2026)

In an office, a person walks over to the regulatory expert and asks "when is that report due?" They do not scan a calendar. So the first tab is a chat: it answers calendar questions from the calendar's own rows and the readings of the documents behind them, with web search for the rule, the way the HR Workspace answers from handbooks. The second tab is the calendar itself, month view by default with a list beside it. Clicking a date opens a drawer with everything about it, nothing hidden. The third tab is the queue: Dates to confirm.

Dates come from three sections today, Documents above all. From now on those sections stop asking the person anything about dates: what they find flows to the one calendar, and the calendar is where a person confirms. Many people will never confirm; the product promised to be a guard, so a date shows on the calendar and in the email whether it is confirmed or not.

The layout is the HR section's, exactly. The words are the product's: never "AI", never "compliant", never "done" or "overdue" (a date has passed), no cost on a customer screen, not legal advice.

29. **The current reading wins (the owner, 15 October, from the step 6 safety check).** A date's identity cannot rest on the model repeating its own words. **Reading a document again withdraws every PROPOSED date from that document's earlier readings that the new reading did not propose again**, and **a finished run of an agency withdraws the proposed audit dates from that agency's earlier runs that this run did not match.** A person's decision outranks a re-read: a confirmed date is never touched, and a date a person dismissed stays dismissed and is never re-proposed (rule b, §2.6). A withdrawn row is **not** a person's dismissal — if a later reading proposes the same title and day again it comes back (the `revived` outcome) — so the two are different words in the DATA, not a distinction in code comments: migration 075 widens the status CHECK to a fourth word and adds `withdrawn_at`. Every reader hides a withdrawn row, and `calendar_v` does it in the view itself so steps 8, 11 and 12 inherit it.

    **The measurement it rests on, not an opinion.** The owner's premise was that the wording drift was a cheap model's fault. It is not: three Opus 5.5 readings of one fire-extinguisher certificate turned three obligations into **nine** live rows — `created 3, existing 0, replaced 0, revived 0`, three times out of three — and the production model also moved a day, reading "schedule before November 2026" as 2026-11-01 once and 2026-10-31 twice. A dearer model read **better** (it found a monthly obligation Haiku missed every time) and no more **steadily**. Nothing in `proposeDate` is wrong: a document date's identity is its title, the title is prose, and `prompts/document-scan.ts` is shown the labels, fact keys, corrections and open gaps **with their ids** — so a new reading can say `same_as_gap_id` — but never the dates already on file and no `same_as_deadline_id`. The model cannot match wording nobody tells it about. **The prompt is the long-term fix and is a separate measured change in Documents** (decision 23's rule: the scan's prompt is not changed here).

    *Effect: Documents — `lib/documentScan.ts` gains one guarded call after the filing loop and a fourth wording in `components/DocumentReport.tsx`; the pre-existing facts rule gains the `could_not_read` guard it never had. Audits — `lib/auditRun.ts`'s `finishRunIfDone` gains one guarded call after its claim, `RunSummary` a `calendar_withdrawn` count, and `components/AuditReport.tsx` a fourth wording. Calendar — `app/api/calendar/route.ts` GET filters withdrawn, which fixes the Dashboard and the old page with it.*
30. **When unsure, ask plainly and politely (the owner, 15 October).** Wherever the product does not know something it says so in one clear sentence and asks: a date read from a month with no day — *"The certificate gives the month, not the day. We've placed it on 30 November; pick the day if you know it"* — a repeating date whose period was not read, a removed site, a document with a newer version, a date the chat could not find. **Never a silent guess shown as read.** It applies to every screen, the monthly email and the chat's prompt. *Effect: a wording rule for steps 7 to 11, and a standing line in every instruction from step 7. Step 6b's two new wordings are written to it: "Not in the latest reading of this file" and "Not in the latest audit of this agency", each a statement with no link, because there is nowhere for the link to go.*

**`calendar_v`** (security_invoker): every column above, plus `site_id` = the document's current site for a document-sourced row (through `document_index_v`), else the row's own `entity_id`; `site_name`; `next_due` = `due_date` for a one-off, the next occurrence of `due_date` by `period` on or after today for a repeating date with a period, null otherwise; `days_until`; `has_passed`; `source_title` (the document's title from `document_index_v`, the handbook's name, the audit's agency and date). Nothing derived is stored.

**`proposeDate(client, input)`** in `lib/calendarDates.ts`: `{ companyId, source, title, dueOn, recurs, description?, sourceLine?, sourceUrl?, sourcePage?, documentId?, deadlineId?, auditFindingId?, handbookId?, entityId?, addedBy?, status? }` → `{ id, outcome: 'created' | 'existing' | 'replaced' | 'revived' }`. Rules: a `document` source with the same `(company_id, document_id, lower(title), due_date)` returns `existing`, even when dismissed; the same title with a different date marks the old row `replaced_by` and creates the new one; `person` and `rule` sources are created `confirmed` with `added_by`; everything else `proposed`. Pure except for the two queries; unit-tested on every branch.

**THE AUDIT NO-DUPLICATES RULE, IN CODE (step 6).** 073's unique index is partial on
`source = 'document'`, so it cannot hold an audit date: every run writes new `audit_findings` rows,
so `audit_finding_id` changes each time the same agency is audited and an index keyed on it would
file one obligation once per audit. The key for an audit date is therefore **(company,
`lower(title)`, `coalesce(due_date)`) among LIVE audit rows** — live meaning `replaced_by` and
`dismissed_by` both null — and a match is `existing` with **one** write: `audit_finding_id` moves to
the newest finding, so the drawer opens the audit a person last ran. A person-dismissed match is
`existing` and **untouched**: re-pointing a row somebody removed would revive it on every screen
that reads the finding. An audit date never matches a document row with the same title and day —
two pieces of evidence for one obligation, and the drawer opens each differently.

**AND THE RULE THAT GOVERNS BOTH FEEDS: a date that cannot be filed must never fail the reading or
the audit that found it.** The scan and the audit are the paid work and are already billed by the
time filing runs; filing is a side effect. Every `proposeDate()` call in `lib/documentScan.ts` and
`lib/auditRun.ts` is wrapped on its own — a failure is logged with the document or finding id,
counted, and the loop carries on, and the reading or run completes exactly as before. The counts go
to the server log for Documents (`document_scans` has no column for them) and to
`audit_runs.summary.calendar_filed` / `calendar_failed` for Audits.

**⚠ OPEN FOR THE OWNER — THE TITLE IS PART OF THE KEY AND THE MODEL DOES NOT KEEP IT STILL.**
Measured on staging, 15 October, three Haiku scans of one fire-extinguisher certificate: **two real
dates became seven calendar rows**, because each reading reworded the obligation ("Next annual
maintenance due for all units" / "Next annual maintenance for all units" / "Annual maintenance on
all extinguishers"), and a new wording is a new key. The same thing happened on the second audit of
one agency (one row re-pointed, one new row from "for all" → "of all"). Neither is a code fault:
`proposeDate` does exactly what this section specifies. It is the **identity** of a date that needs
deciding, and three ways out are set down in `DECISIONS.md` §187 — the one consistent with decisions
13 and 14 being that **the current reading wins**: when a scan finishes, any unconfirmed
document-sourced row for that document which this reading did not propose is superseded, the way
`document_gaps` is already marked `not_seen_at`. Step 9 (Dates to confirm) is where a person meets
the consequence, so the decision is wanted before it.

**Two more rules and a fourth outcome, the owner, 15 October (built in step 5).** (a) A document-sourced row that was only SUPERSEDED — `replaced_by` set, `dismissed_by` null — and that a later reading proposes again **comes back**: status `proposed`, `replaced_by` cleared, and the row that had replaced it marked replaced by this one. The outcome is **`revived`**, because the latest reading of a document is the truth. (b) A row a PERSON dismissed — `dismissed_by` set — **never comes back**, whatever a reading says; the outcome is `existing` and nothing is written. A dismissal therefore outranks a superseding. The invariant both rules keep, which no constraint can see: **at most one LIVE row per (company, document, lower(title))**, live meaning `dismissed_by is null and replaced_by is null`. Readers filter on `replaced_by is null`. `description` is in the signature too — the person's own note, distinct from `sourceLine`, which is the sentence a document set the date in.
