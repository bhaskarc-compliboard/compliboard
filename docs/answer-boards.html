The answer and its source cards

Draft 3, 5 November 2026 — no kind labels; the follow-up box; the offer asks for documents. One board, one answer, drawn in the Audits chat so every kind of source appears: an audit finding, a verified quote, a named document with no quote, a web page. The same component draws the answer on seven surfaces — the Workspace, HR, the Calendar, Documents, Audits, the conversation drawer and the report — so what is decided here lands everywhere at once.

Nothing about what the model writes changes. E3 is how an answer is laid out, how its four kinds of source are told apart, how an honesty note looks, and how an offer looks. Alignment (decision 59) holds: number, title and kind in fixed columns down the list.

Top to bottom. The headline in the reading face, then the body with small green citation marks as today. An honesty note — the "could not match the file's words" line, the "Queued" line, "readings as of" — drawn as a quiet grey note with a mark, never as a sentence inside the answer. The offer as a green card with two buttons: run now, or add a folder first — and it says how many documents are on file for that agency and what adding more would let the audit answer (the owner, 5 November: the offer is one way documents come in, and an audit's worth of files is a folder). "Add a folder first" goes to the Documents tab's folder picker. Then Sources: one card per source in a fixed grid — number · title and origin · a kind pill on the right — and the verified quote in the reading italic under it when there is one. No kind labels (the owner, 5 November): a card with a quote under it is checked, a card without one is a reference, and the origin line already says whose it is — "your document", "your audit", "osha.oregon.gov". An audit finding gets a thin amber left edge so it reads as a different kind of thing without a word. Every title is the link, opening in place where the section has a drawer. Then the box for the follow-up question and the action row, exactly where they are today.

Decided (proposed) 5 November. A quote and a name look different, because a quote is proof and a name is a reference; a person scanning the pills sees how much of the answer is checked. Honesty lines look like notes, not like the answer. The offer looks like a button, not like prose.
Your reading. The honesty note: grey box as drawn, or a plain line. And the audit finding's amber edge: keep or drop.
